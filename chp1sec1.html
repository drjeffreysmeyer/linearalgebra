    Section 1.3 Uniqueness of Identities and Inverses

    In this investigation, you now revisit basic algebraic properties of \(\R\text{,}\) properties you may have taken for granted, but you cannot do so for its abstraction. You will practice deducing these basis properties from field axioms.
    🔗
    In Definition 1.2.1 we assume identity elements and inverses exist, we do not assume they are unique.
    🔗

    Proposition 1.3.1 Additive identities are unique.

    If \(F\) is a field and \(0_1,0_2\in F\) both satisfy the property that, for all \(a\in F\text{,}\) \(0_1+a=a\) and \(0_2+a=a\text{,}\) then \(0_1=0_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Hint.
    Try a direct proof. Analyze the sum \(0_1+0_2\text{.}\) You may find field property (F4) in Definition 1.2.1 useful.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 1.3.2 Multiplicative identities are unique.

    If \(F\) is a field and \(1_1,1_2\in F\) both satisfy the property that, for all \(a\in F\text{,}\) \(1_1a=a\) and \(1_2a=a\text{,}\) then \(1_1=1_2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Hint.
    Have you seen a similar result whose proof you may leverage?
    🔗
    🔗
    We are therefore justified in discussing the identity element.
    🔗

    Proposition 1.3.3 Additive inverses are unique.

    If \(F\) is a field, \(a\in F\text{,}\) and \(b,c\in F\) satisfy the property that \(a+b=0\) and \(a+c=0\text{,}\) then \(b=c\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Hint.
    Try a direct proof. Analyze the sum \(b+a+c\) in two ways. Remember, addition is a binary operation, so which pair do you add first? You may find field properties (F1), (F2) in Definition 1.2.1 useful.
    🔗
    🔗

    Proposition 1.3.4 Multiplicative inverses are unique.

    If \(F\) is a field, \(a\in F^\times\text{,}\) and \(b,c\in F^\times\) satisfy the property that \(ab=1\) and \(ac=1\text{,}\) then \(b=c\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Hint.
    Have you seen a similar result whose proof you may leverage?
    🔗
    🔗
    We are therefore justified in discussing the inverse. Going forward, we will use the notation \(-a\) to denote the additive inverse of \(a\in F\) and \(a^{-1}\) to denote the multiplicative inverse of \(a\in F^\times\text{.}\)
