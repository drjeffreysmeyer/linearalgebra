Definition 3.7.1 Coordinates.

If \(V\) is a vector space with ordered basis \(\mathcal{B}=\{\vect{v}_1,\ldots, \vect{v}_n\}\text{,}\) and \(\vect{w}\in V\text{,}\) then by Proposition 2.13.4, there exists unique scalars \(x_1,\ldots, x_n\in F\) such that \(\vect{w}=x_1\vect{v}_1+\cdots +x_n\vect{v}_n\text{.}\) This collection of scalars is the coordinates of \(\vect{w}\) relative to the ordered basis \(\mathcal{B}\text{.}\) We collect them in the \(\mathcal{B}\)-coordinate vector
🔗
\begin{equation*} [\vect{w}]_\mathcal{B} := \begin{bmatrix}x_1\\x_2\\ \vdots \\ x_n\end{bmatrix} \end{equation*}
The set map
🔗
\begin{equation*} \Coord_\mathcal{B}:V\to F^n, \quad \vect{w}\mapsto [\vect{w}]_\mathcal{B} \end{equation*}
is the \(\mathcal{B}\)-coordinate map, or more precisely, the map into \(\mathcal{B}\)-coordinates.
